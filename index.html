01 / RESEARCH CONTEXT

A small dataset shouldn't dictate a small search.

Gas turbine anomaly detection is a binary classification problem with limited abnormal examples. Choosing a network topology by hand risks underfitting or overfitting the available sensor data.

CAE–WANN extends the feature search space with a convolutional auto-encoder, then searches neural network architectures within that representation. A maximum-likelihood threshold adapts the final decision to imbalanced anomaly scores.

LEARNING STRATEGY

One sequential path. One feedback loop.

The sequential procedure trains the CAE before WANN search. The alternating procedure updates the representation and topology in turn, allowing classification feedback to reshape latent features.

The CAE combines reconstruction loss with WANN feedback. Architecture search balances prediction loss against network complexity; the searched network uses roughly 160 connections compared with 810 in the BPNN baseline.

03 / EXPERIMENTAL EVIDENCE

Higher AUC. A more considered threshold.

On 155 test windows—130 normal and 25 abnormal—overall accuracy alone would be misleading. The study therefore examines AUC, sensitivity (TPR), specificity (TNR) and the Youden index.

MODEL COMPARISON

Performance without unnecessary complexity.

At shared weight +1, CAE–WANN reaches 86.74% mean AUC—1.84 percentage points above the best WANN setting and 6.97 points above BPNN.

The searched model has about 160 connections rather than BPNN's 810. Inference for all 155 test samples takes 2.81 ms, compared with 0.07 ms for decision-tree baselines.

TRAINING DYNAMICS

A stable search trajectory.

The best WANN's losses stabilize after roughly 512 generations. CAE reconstruction also converges across tested channel widths; the study selects a two- and four-channel configuration.

OPERATING THRESHOLD

A decision boundary that respects both classes.

Fixed threshold selection favors detecting abnormalities (91.54% TPR), while an adaptive threshold favors normal classification (92.00% TNR). MLETS balances these objectives at 85.38% TPR and 84.00% TNR.

On the held-out test set, MLETS correctly identifies 111 of 130 normal windows and 21 of 25 abnormal windows.
